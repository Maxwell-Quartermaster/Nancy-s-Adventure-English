# R001: Collectible character shirt sets, research for the WICQA five-bot shirts

**Quartermaster.** Filed Mon Nov 2, 2026, about 10:20 PM MST. Answers Maxwell t749u, plus the follow-up asks in t750u (what sold, which exclusivity play to use, the numbered-drop mechanic).
**Method.** WebSearch and WebFetch only. I opened every link below or checked it with curl on Nov 2, 2026, and dropped anything returning a 404 or error (for example, a Milan numbered-tee drop page that returned a 500). There were no accounts, no purchases and no contact with anyone.
**Rules followed.** No sales figure is invented: if a source doesn't give a number, this report says "unknown". Uncertain points are marked **[uncertain]**. This is research, not legal advice.

The WICQA spec this report is checked against (CURRENT-v1): five original bots (Quartermaster, Watchman, Chancellor, Instructor, Auditor), one shirt each, lining up as W-I-C-Q-A. Only the bot's name and art go on each shirt, and the WICQA label is never printed (t738u). The palette is purple and neon green. The front has one shared tiny polo-style badge (a quarter-machine ninja-figure vibe) on the wearer's right chest. The back has a square border with the character staged inside. The look is Pixar/Play-Doh. Sales go through Etsy with Printful POD. Embroidery comes later.

## 1. Examples

The "What sold" lines repeat only what the source says.

### E1. Smiski (Dreams Inc., Japan): a small set with one secret
- **What it is.** Tiny glow-in-the-dark green figures sold in blind boxes. Each series has about 6 poses plus one "secret" figure. Older series run 1 in 144 boxes for the secret; newer ones are easier (1 in 72, 1 in 36, 1 in 12). Some retired series (3 and 4) still trade among collectors.
- **Marketing.** One consistent body shape, a funny everyday pose for each figure, a backstory that they hide in the corners of your house, and TikTok unboxing.
- **What sold.** Unknown. None of the sources I opened gives unit or dollar numbers.
- Sources: https://blinkbox.com.au/blogs/box-of-wonders/smiski-secret-figure-guide and https://theconversation.com/labubus-sonny-angels-and-smiskis-are-blind-toy-boxes-just-childs-play-or-something-more-concerning-257611
- **WICQA takeaway.** A shared silhouette plus a signature pose per character is exactly what makes a set feel collectible. One caution **[uncertain]**: a neon-green, rounded, Play-Doh mascot could read as Smiski-like, so keep the bots' shapes distinct.

### E2. Sonny Angel (Dreams Inc.): themed series and a rare chase
- **What it is.** Small cherub figures in themed blind-box series, with secret figures and a rare "Robby Angel" chase.
- **Marketing.** Gen Z collectors, unboxing videos, shelf displays. The Conversation explains repeat buying as variable-ratio reward ("you never know which one you'll get").
- **What sold.** Unknown. No figures in the opened sources.
- Source: https://theconversation.com/labubus-sonny-angels-and-smiskis-are-blind-toy-boxes-just-childs-play-or-something-more-concerning-257611
- **WICQA takeaway.** Blind-box chance mechanics drive the buying, but they don't carry over to POD shirts, because a buyer picks a specific shirt. What does carry over is the themed series and the idea of a rare one.

### E3. Pop Mart's THE MONSTERS / LABUBU on UNIQLO UT tees: a toy craze moving into apparel
- **What it is.** UNIQLO UT T-shirts and sweatshirts with Labubu and the other MONSTERS characters by artist Kasing Lung. In Japan: 4 women's tees and 3 girls' sweatshirts.
- **Marketing.** Riding the toy craze. A one-week online reorder window (Sep 1–7, 2025), a limit of 2 per design per person, and the window closing early once a cap was hit.
- **What sold.** Fashion Press says the Aug 25, 2025 release sold out almost as soon as it launched, which is why the reorder window opened. Unit numbers are unknown. Separately, CNN reports Pop Mart's profit rose almost 400% in the first half of 2025 (company-wide, not shirts).
- Sources: https://www.fashion-press.net/news/137589 (Japanese), https://www.cnn.com/2025/09/05/china/china-lafufu-copy-crackdown-intl-hnk. The UNIQLO US collection page https://www.uniqlo.com/us/en/special-feature/ut/pop-mart loaded through WebFetch but returns 403 to curl.
- **WICQA takeaway.** The shirts sold because the characters were already loved. The love comes first and the shirt follows, which argues for building the bots' audience (dashboard, Pi robots, stickers) alongside the shirts.

### E4. "Lafufu" counterfeits: the copy problem at full scale
- **What it is.** Knockoff Labubu dolls, usually about a tenth of the official price.
- **What sold.** One Dongguan factory told CNN it sold 150,000–160,000 fakes at the July 2025 peak, and that a "Lagogo" variant sells 30,000–40,000 a month. Chinese authorities seized over 1.8 million dolls headed for export. Pop Mart filed "Lafufu" as a defensive trademark.
- Source: https://www.cnn.com/2025/09/05/china/china-lafufu-copy-crackdown-intl-hnk
- **WICQA takeaway.** Any hit design gets copied. Scarcity that's too extreme (sold out everywhere) pushes buyers toward fakes. For a tiny POD seller the threat is art theft (see F3), not factories.

### E5. Chiikawa × GU (Japan): a small cast, limits, and resale
- **What it is.** Apparel and socks featuring the Chiikawa characters (a small core trio).
- **Marketing.** A limit of 2 per design per color per person, plus a public warning against buying to resell.
- **What sold.** FASHIONSNAP reports that on launch day (May 23, 2025) some sock colors had sold out by 14:00 on the online store, many items had missing sizes, and flea-market resale listings were near 3x retail. Units are unknown.
- Source: https://www.fashionsnap.com/article/2025-05-23/gu-chiikawa-headsup/ (Japanese)
- **WICQA takeaway.** A small cast where each character has a clear personality is enough to drive "I need mine". You don't need 50 characters. Five is a good number.

### E6. BT21 (LINE Friends × BTS): one character per member plus a mascot for the set
- **What it is.** Seven characters, each sketched by a BTS member (Tata/V, RJ/Jin, Chimmy/Jimin, Cooky/Jungkook, Mang/J-Hope, Shooky/Suga, Koya/RM), plus "Van", a robot representing the band and its fans. The line covers stickers first, then plush, figures and apparel.
- **Marketing.** Behind-the-scenes videos of the members designing their characters. LINE Friends' CEO said apparel would be "a walking billboard" and, for the buyer, "a show of identity." The line was aimed beyond fans, so people could discover later that "there is BTS behind these characters."
- **What sold.** Billboard (Oct 2017) says the stickers were downloaded over 8 million times, with 71 million Twitter exposures, before the merch launched. Apparel sales are unknown.
- Sources: https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/bts-line-friends-bt21-collab-new-fashion-emojis-k-pop-8006877/ and https://bt21.com/about
- **WICQA takeaway.** This is the closest match to WICQA: one character per team member, plus one shared symbol for the group. Van is like the WICQA shared badge. Its story ("each member designed their own") is like "each bot has a job in Maxwell's life." A free character asset launched first (stickers) built the audience before apparel.

### E7. Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: the classic five-color team
- **What it is.** Five color-coded heroes (Red, Blue, Black, Yellow, Pink).
- **Marketing.** For the 2025 Playmates relaunch, the packaging uses a stack of all five helmets on every box, and each character's color helps buyers find their favorite. Notably, the overall brand colors are bright green with a purple disc, almost exactly the WICQA purple and neon green.
- **What sold.** Unknown for this line. The Toy Book piece is about design, not sales.
- Source: https://toybook.com/outside-the-box-re-igniting-power-rangers/
- **WICQA takeaway.** Show all five together on every listing (as the helmet stack does) so the missing ones are obvious. Use one shared brand palette (purple/green) plus one accent color per character.

### E8. Piapro Characters (Hatsune Miku, Rin, Len, Luka, MEIKO, KAITO): a small color-coded cast
- **What it is.** Six characters, each with an official signature color, sold as sets (pin sets, plush sets) and as apparel.
- **What sold.** Unknown.
- Source: https://piapro.net/intl/en_character.html
- **WICQA takeaway.** This is more proof that a set of 5 or 6 with one color per character is a proven collectible format. Once Maxwell has chosen them, each bot should get a single official accent color and keep it everywhere.

### E9. Kidrobot Dunny Series 2011: an artist series with odds and "Collect and Connect"
- **What it is.** A blind-box vinyl series by many artists, with published odds (Huck Gee's Zombie Hunter at 1/200). Each box held one piece of a Skeleton Dunny by Kronk, and "it's not complete until you've found the figure's heart."
- **Marketing.** Release parties in stores and printable official checklists.
- **What sold.** Unknown.
- Source: https://www.theblotsays.com/2011/06/kidrobot-dunny-series-2011-official.html
- **WICQA takeaway.** The **collect-and-connect** idea fits WICQA naturally. The five back designs could be framed so the borders join, or a sixth scene only appears when all five are laid side by side, rewarding the full set without any chance mechanics.

### E10. TeeFury: 24-hour indie artist shirt drops
- **What it is.** Since 2008, a new design every 24 hours at a discounted price. Afterwards it moves to the Gallery at regular price, and fans can vote to bring designs back.
- **What sold.** TeeFury's About page says artists are paid $1 per shirt sold during the sale and that artists have earned "anywhere from $500 to over $9,000 in a single day for 1 shirt". That's TeeFury's own claim, and the typical result is unknown.
- Sources: https://teefury.com/pages/about and https://teefury.gorgias.help/en-US/what-are-%22daily%22-designs-7603
- **WICQA takeaway.** A time-boxed window plus a regular-price afterlife is honest and works with POD. One caution: the shirt isn't really retired, so the "limited" part is the launch price, not the design.

### E11. Cotton Bureau: two-week preorder campaigns
- **What it is.** Screen-printed shirts sold in a two-week preorder window, with a 12-shirt minimum to print. There's also an always-on on-demand option.
- **What sold.** Cotton Bureau's own blog says "the truth is, most shirts don't make it", meaning without active promotion they don't hit the minimum. CBS Pittsburgh reports the company ships about 4,000 shirts a month (company-wide).
- Sources: https://cottonbureau.com/how-it-works, https://blog.cottonbureau.com/how-to-sell-25-shirts-in-two-weeks/, https://blog.cottonbureau.com/get-back-to-where-we-once-belonged/, https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/strip-district-company-prints-t-shirts-by-the-people-for-the-people/
- **WICQA takeaway.** A drop window only works if you promote it every other day during the window. Silent drops fail.

### E12. Tangerine London: numbered, retired artist editions
- **What it is.** Each artwork is printed on exactly 300 numbered shirts and never reprinted. The artwork is "retired and entered in our public register." There's a scannable "Provenance" record in the hem with the artist, the piece and the number. Their opening release is a set of **five** pieces. A plain "Essentials" line stays always in stock.
- **What sold.** Unknown, and the brand looks new, so its track record is unproven **[uncertain]**.
- Source: https://tangerinelondon.com/pages/faq
- **WICQA takeaway.** This is the cleanest model of the "unique artist" play: fixed edition size, per-unit number, public register, no restock, plus a separate always-available line. It relies on in-house printing (they print exactly 300), which is not how Printful POD works. See section 5.

### E13 (bonus). Pusheen: an indie character that grew from comics to stickers to merch
- **What it is.** A 2010 webcomic cat by Claire Belton and Andrew Duff that went viral on Tumblr, then became a Facebook chat sticker in 2013.
- **What sold.** Pusheen Corp says it works with more than 100 licensees and estimates Pusheen images have been sent "more than 20 billion times" (the company's own estimate). Apparel sales are unknown.
- Source: https://pusheen.com/pages/about
- **WICQA takeaway.** A one- or two-person indie character can become a brand if the character is shared freely first (stickers, GIFs) and the merch follows.

## 2. What worked

1. **A small cast with one clear personality and signature pose each** (Smiski, Chiikawa, BT21, Power Rangers). The pose or role is the reason someone picks "theirs" first.
2. **One shared visual system plus one color per character** (Power Rangers helmets, Piapro colors). The set looks like a set, and a missing piece is obvious.
3. **A shared symbol for the whole group** (BT21's Van). The WICQA shared front badge already does this.
4. **An origin story** ("each member drew their own", BT21). For WICQA: "five bots that each run a part of one maker's life, drawn by that maker."
5. **Building love before selling the shirt** (Pusheen and BT21 stickers first; Labubu toys first, then the UNIQLO tees).
6. **Honest windows and limits** (TeeFury's 24 hours, Cotton Bureau's 2 weeks, UNIQLO and GU's 2-per-person limits) create urgency that's actually true.
7. **Rewarding the full set without gambling** (the Dunny collect-and-connect piece).
8. **Clear edition terms** (Tangerine: exact size, number, no restock, public register).

## 3. What flopped (and why)

- **F1. Beanie Babies, 1999: engineered "retirement" scarcity collapsed.** Ty announced retirements to push prices up. In 1999 a retirement announcement produced no price jump, collectors panic-sold, and prices never recovered. Fortune cites author Zac Bissonnette's estimate that surviving Beanie Babies are worth about 50 cents each, and says that at one point they were about 10% of eBay's sales. Source: https://fortune.com/2015/03/11/beanie-babies-failure-lessons/ (also https://www.marketplace.org/story/2023/08/10/why-the-beanie-baby-bubble-burst). **Lesson:** scarcity sold as an *investment* bubbles and bursts. Sell WICQA shirts as things to love and wear, never as things that will go up in value.
- **F2. NFT "collectibles": artificial digital scarcity.** dappGambl's 2023 analysis claimed about 95% of NFT collections had a market cap of zero. **[uncertain]** That study was criticized as non-peer-reviewed with shaky methods. Sources: https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/sep/22/nfts-worthless-price and the critique at https://riatlabs.com/2023/10/03/misguided-metrics-how-the-dead-nft-study-reflects-bias-propagates-false-data-and-fuels-opinionated-media/. **Lesson:** a numbered "edition" of something infinitely copyable can feel hollow. The number needs to sit on a real object with a real cap.
- **F3. POD art theft by bots.** The BBC (2019) reported bots scanning Twitter for comments like "I want this on a T-shirt", grabbing the artwork, listing it on shirt sites and replying with the link, sometimes with no human involved. Artists described sending DMCA notices over and over, and offshore sites often ignored them. Sources: https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-50817561 and https://gizmodo.com/i-want-that-on-a-t-shirt-1840273177. Etsy's report path: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000344448-How-to-Report-Intellectual-Property-Infringement. **Lesson:** with POD, "nobody else has it" can't be guaranteed. The art can be stolen, so the moat has to be the artist, the story and the official channel, not the image file.
- **F4. Unpromoted drop campaigns.** Cotton Bureau itself says most campaigns don't reach the minimum without steady promotion (E11). **Lesson:** a time-boxed drop with no audience is just a listing that disappears.
- **F5. Too much scarcity breeds fakes and backlash** (E4 Lafufu: CNN quotes a buyer calling knockoffs "my way of rebelling" against inflated prices). **Lesson:** keep the core shirts available at a fair price. Put the scarcity only on an extra layer.

## 4. Exclusivity vs collect-all-five: which fits POD

| | A. "Collect all five" (open edition) | B. "Unique artist, nobody else has it" (limited/numbered) |
|---|---|---|
| How it fits POD | **Natural.** Printful prints forever at no inventory risk. | **Awkward.** POD can print unlimited copies, and the cap is only your promise to close the listing. |
| What drives buying | Love of the characters plus completing the set (each purchase makes the next more tempting). | Status, urgency, and owning something by the artist. |
| Revenue shape | Steady and repeatable, and every new fan can still buy all five. | Spikes, then nothing. A sell-out you can't restock. |
| Risks | Can feel generic if the art isn't strong. Copycats. | Honesty rules (Etsy, FTC) if the cap isn't real. Beanie/NFT backlash. Unsold editions look bad ("37 of 100" left forever). |
| Cost to run | Low. | Higher: tracking numbers, pack-ins, closing listings on time. |

**Recommendation: a blend, led by collect-all-five.**
1. **Base layer, collect-all-five, always available.** The five bot shirts are open edition, fairly priced, shown together everywhere, with an "all five" bundle listing. This matches what POD does well, and it's how Smiski, BT21 and Power Rangers actually make their money.
2. **The artist layer is your story, not a scarcity claim.** "Original characters drawn by Maxwell in Jackson Hole" and the official-shop line go in every listing and the shop About section. This is the honest version of "nobody else has it": the only *official* source, not "the only copy."
3. **A thin exclusivity layer, time-boxed and truly retired.** Run one **"First Run"** variant per season, such as a launch-only alternate back colorway or badge variant, sold in a hard window (for example 7 days). Then really delete it and never reprint, and log it in a public "retired designs" list. This copies TeeFury and Tangerine honestly, without needing per-unit numbering from Printful.

Why the blend wins: pure B on POD depends on promises the factory can't enforce and carries the Beanie/NFT backlash risk. Pure A gives up the urgency that makes launches move. The blend keeps the main income steady and adds launch-day urgency that is true.

## 5. The numbered-drop mechanic (for example "001 of 100")

**Does a number help or hurt?**
- *Helps:* it turns a shirt into an object you can own, it gives buyers a story ("I'm #007"), and the Tangerine model shows that buyers understand it.
- *Hurts:* the number only means something if the cap is real and enforced. Unsold numbers look weak. Under F1 and F2, numbering a shirt anyone could print again feels hollow. Fulfillment with POD is messy (below).

**Can Printful print a different number on each shirt (inside or outside label)? Not in any feature I found.**
- Printful's custom inside label is one design (your logo area) per product template. The size, material, origin and care text fills in automatically per variant "and you can't make edits manually." It costs $0.99–$1.25 per garment, the logo area is 3″ × 1.13″, and it only works on blanks with tear-away tags. Sources: https://www.printful.com/custom-inside-labels and https://www.printful.com/blog/printful-inside-outside-labels. A Printful staffer said on Reddit that they used to allow fully custom labels but removed the option (https://www.reddit.com/r/printful/comments/jr7o8x/custom_inside_label/). The help center article https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/50264168090385-Can-I-print-custom-neck-labels loaded through search but returns 403 to curl; it says outside labels can be any design, and you can't combine inside and outside labels on one shirt.
- **Conclusion [uncertain, inferred]:** the design is fixed per product, so there's no per-unit numbering. The only workaround would be 100 separate products, one per number, which is impractical and makes for an Etsy listing mess. Not recommended.

**The cheaper alternative: a numbered card as a pack-in.**
- Printful pack-ins allow up to 6″ × 8″ × 1″ and 2 oz. Cost is a $0.50 picking fee per pack-in plus a $25 monthly storage minimum. **Catch:** if an order ships from a facility that doesn't stock your cards, "your order will ship without them." Source: https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/50265475398161-Can-I-add-promotional-materials-to-my-orders (fetched with WebFetch) and https://www.printful.com/branded-packaging-inserts. Product storage ended Mar 1, 2026, but storage for branding materials continues.
- So pre-numbered cards would go out in arbitrary order, and some orders could miss a card. You can't promise "you'll get #001". The listing could say "includes a numbered First Run card (number assigned at random; while cards last)" **[uncertain: wording is a suggestion, not checked by Etsy]**.
- An even cheaper option needs no Printful setup: a free **digital** numbered certificate (a PDF or a page on Maxwell's site listing owner #s), sent after purchase. Or Maxwell mails a numbered sticker himself to First Run buyers, which costs postage and his time.

**Etsy and US honesty rules for "limited edition" claims**
- Etsy lets you sell POD only under "Designed by a seller." You must disclose Printful as a production partner on each listing, and **seller-prompted AI creations must disclose the use of AI**. Sources: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024112614-What-Can-I-Sell-on-Etsy and https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336547-Working-with-Production-Partners-on-Etsy. **Flag for Maxwell/Chancellor:** if any final mascot art is AI-generated or AI-assisted, the listings must say so.
- I found **no Etsy rule written specifically about "limited edition"** **[uncertain]**. Etsy's Seller Policy requires listings to accurately represent items and how they're made. I couldn't open https://www.etsy.com/legal/sellers/ directly (403 and timeout); that wording comes from the search summary. Seller guides say limited claims should state the edition size and number (https://blog.storescore.dev/shop-presentation-checklist-for-art-sellers-on-etsy/).
- The US FTC's 2022 "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" report lists **false low-stock messages, countdown timers that reset, and "limited time" offers with no real deadline** as deceptive. Source: https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **Rule of thumb:** say a real number ("First Run, open Nov X–Y, then retired"), keep the promise (delete the listing and the Printful product on the end date), and never reopen the same edition.

**⚠ Conflict to flag, not assume.** A tag or card reading "**WICQA** 001 of 100" would print the brand as a label, which Maxwell ruled out in t738u ("The brand is WICQA but we never put the label on there, just the bot names"). Options for Maxwell to choose from:
- (a) The tag carries the bot name and number only, for example "WATCHMAN · First Run · 007".
- (b) The number goes only on a pack-in card or digital certificate, not on the shirt. Maxwell still has to decide whether a card *in the box* may say WICQA.
- (c) The badge mascot or a symbol stands in for the brand, with no word on it.
The same question applies to the inside label logo: the t738u rule suggests it should show the badge figure, not the word WICQA. That's Maxwell's call.

## 6. Recommendations for WICQA (concrete, cheap, numbered)

1. **Lead with collect-all-five, open edition.** Five listings plus one "full set of five" listing (Chancellor already logged this shape for Maxwell to pick). Cost: $0 beyond the listings.
2. **Show all five side by side on every listing image** (the Power Rangers helmet-stack logic), with the current shirt highlighted. Buyers see what's missing. Cost: one mockup template.
3. **One accent color per bot, inside the purple and neon-green system.** Lock five accent colors once and reuse them everywhere (Piapro, Power Rangers). Chancellor's Printful color matching already covers this.
4. **Signature pose and one-line job per bot** in each listing ("Watchman keeps the lantern lit"), the Smiski/BT21 personality hook. Keep shapes distinct from Smiski's green blob.
5. **Collect-and-connect backs.** Design the five square-bordered backs so their border art joins into one wide scene when laid side by side, a full-set reward with no chance mechanics (Dunny). Cost: design time only. Add it to the brief as an idea for Maxwell to approve.
6. **The shared front badge is the group symbol** (BT21's Van). Keep it identical on all five, as Maxwell already decided in t747u.
7. **"First Run" seasonal drop.** One limited variant (alternate back colorway or badge), a 7-day window, then truly deleted and logged in a public "retired designs" list. Use Printify/Printful-style scheduled publishing for the start (Printify's drop guide, which recommends hard 24–72 hour windows and never restocking: https://printify.com/knowledge-hub/printify-product-drops-strategy-guide/). Promote it every other day during the window (Cotton Bureau's lesson).
8. **Numbering, if wanted, goes off-garment first:** a free digital certificate, or a Printful pack-in card ($0.50 each plus $25 a month) with honest wording about random numbers and cards possibly missing. Skip per-unit printed numbers on POD.
9. **Free character content before and alongside the shirts:** sticker sheets, GIFs, and the dashboard bots on public pages (Pusheen and BT21 built audiences first).
10. **Protect the art:** keep the master files and dated originals in this archive (already done), watermark social previews, avoid "I want this on a shirt" reply chains, link only to the official Etsy shop, and use Etsy's IP reporting if copies appear. Watchman's screen found WICQA clear (USPTO-WICQA-GROK.md); keep "Grok" out of every design, title and tag.
11. **Honesty checklist on every listing:** Printful disclosed as production partner, AI disclosure if any AI art is used, real dates for any drop, no fake "only 2 left" messages.
12. **Sell the shirts as things to love, never as investments.** No "will go up in value" language (Beanie Babies, NFTs).

## Top recommendations (summary)

- **Blend, led by collect-all-five.** The five bot shirts stay open edition and always available. That's what POD does well.
- **The artist story is the exclusivity.** "Original characters by Maxwell, official shop only" is true. "Nobody else has it" can't be guaranteed with POD.
- **Add one honest "First Run" drop per season.** A 7-day window, then truly retired and logged publicly. No fake timers or restocks.
- **Reward the full set by design.** The five back borders join into one scene, and all five are shown together on every listing.
- **No per-unit numbered labels on Printful.** Their label is one design per product. If Maxwell wants numbers, use a digital certificate or a pack-in card, worded honestly.
- **Flag for Maxwell:** any "WICQA 001/100" tag or label conflicts with t738u. Choose bot name plus number, a number on the card only, or the badge symbol instead.
- **Compliance:** disclose Printful and any AI use on Etsy, and keep "Grok" out of everything.
