# WICQA Party Art Style Guide (v1, Nov 3, 2026)

**Quartermaster:** Every party bot is a simple, made-up cartoon animal hero, not a robot and not a human. They're drawn as if one artist did them all, in a bold, angular, faceted style: big flat color planes with hard-edged cel shading (one light tone and one shadow tone per surface, no gradients), fur, feathers and cloth shown as a few large angular shapes rather than strands or texture, and a clean, even dark outline of medium weight. Each figure stands out against a near-black background with a thin colored rim glow (purple, or the bot's own color). Proportions are compact and heroic: a big head with large, simple expressive eyes (or opaque lenses where the design hides them), a small visible mouth or beak set in a serious, determined line, and a slim-to-medium build. Only Quartermaster is a little broader, and never chunky. Poses are dynamic, with light squash and stretch in the stance (a weight shift, a lean, a reach), and each bot shows one clear signature action, like aiming, pointing, reading or guarding. Costumes are simple: a hood, robe, coat or tabard in one main color with thin gold trim, and at most one or two props. No utility belts, waistbands, buckles or extra straps. The faith motifs stay small and respectful (the eternal-flame lantern, Aaron's rod, the Jerusalem cross). Keep detail low, Minions-level simple, so all five read clearly at small sizes and on shirts. No text in the art.

**Quartermaster:** How to use this guide: render with the three approved solos as the only style references, and never use an off-style older draft as a reference, because edit mode keeps the old style. Then check the result for belts, straps, visible eyes on Auditor, and texture creep.
